Introduction

It's likely that you've come across the quote, "If anything can go wrong, it will." It's often used as an idication of defeat in the face of adversity, but did you know that its original context was never intended to incite submission into preordained calamity but rather a reminder on the importance of preparation?

Murphy's law was first cited by Edward A Murphy Jr, who was an aerospace engineer in the 1940s. The term was coined, not to show acceptance f the fact that things naturally tend towards disorder, but rather to escape this chaos as best as one can through precaution and sensitivity. The existence of this law is not tolerance to all things going wrong — it is evidence that with readiness, many potential setbacks can be avoided.
